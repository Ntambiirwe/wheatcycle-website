01 · The challenge

A waste problem — and a missed opportunity.

After harvest, wheat residue can be burned or discarded when farmers do not have a convenient, trusted reuse pathway. The project explores whether this material can be connected to a practical bedding product.

What we observed

During travel through Punjab, the team observed wheat-crop residue being burned in several farm fields. Interviewed farmers said they were not aware of product uses such as mattresses, and described burning as the quickest, lowest-cost disposal option.

The opportunity is not only to design a mattress material — it is to create a simple route from farm residue to useful value.

04 · Prototype design

Inside the layered wheat-straw pad.

The proposed low-fidelity prototype uses prepared straw enclosed within protective layers. The design aims to be washable, repairable and easy to refill.

Four-part construction

01 · Washable, breathable outer cover
02 · Protective inner lining
03 · Dried, chopped wheat-straw filling
04 · Simple supporting base

Stitched sections help distribute filling. A zip or removable panel can make refilling and repair easier.

Proposed working principle

  1. Collect wheat residue after harvest.
  2. Sort, clean and dry it fully.
  3. Chop or screen the material.
  4. Fill the lining sections evenly.
  5. Close the cover and inspect the pad.
  6. Open sections later to refill or repair.
Safety focus: keep the straw dry and enclosed. Moisture and mould are key concerns.
05 · Test and improve

A prototype must earn trust.

The following are proposed tests, not completed or certified results. Testing would guide the next design iteration.

Comfort and trust

Review softness, support, smell and cover feel. Aim for acceptable comfort and user confidence.

Compression

Apply a known load and compare thickness before and after. Look for limited permanent flattening.

Moisture and mould

Observe under controlled humidity. Check for dampness, strong odour or mould growth.

Dust and enclosure

Inspect after handling to check that filling stays inside the protective lining.

Durability

Repeat loading and inspect seams, cover and sections for damage.

Process test

Record time and effort for collection, drying and filling to identify difficult steps.

Project boundary: this is a proposed student prototype, not a certified sleeping product. It should not be used as a finished mattress without appropriate material, hygiene, fire-safety and durability testing.
